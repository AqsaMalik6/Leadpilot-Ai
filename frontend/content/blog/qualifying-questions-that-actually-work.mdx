A qualifying conversation only earns its place in your funnel if it filters out the leads that shouldn't take up a rep's calendar, without filtering out the ones that should. That balance comes down to which questions you ask, and how.

## The five questions worth asking

1. **What's prompting you to look into this right now?** This surfaces the actual need, and often signals urgency without asking about urgency directly.
2. **Do you have a budget range in mind?** Framed as a range rather than an exact number, this rarely feels invasive and quickly filters out mismatches on both ends.
3. **What's your ideal timeline to get started?** Distinguishes "researching for next year" from "need this live this month" — two very different conversations for a rep to have.
4. **How large is your team, or what's your lead/customer volume?** A rough sizing question that maps a lead onto the right plan or offer before a rep has to guess.
5. **Are you the one making the final call on this, or is someone else involved?** Identifies whether you're talking to an economic buyer, an influencer, or someone doing early research — without it feeling like an interrogation.

## Order and framing matter more than the question list

The same five questions asked as a rapid-fire checklist feel like a form; asked as a natural back-and-forth, they feel like a conversation. A few practical notes:

- Ask **need** first — it's the easiest to answer honestly and builds momentum for the questions that follow.
- Save **budget** for the middle of the conversation, once some context exists, rather than opening with it.
- Only ask **decision authority** if it hasn't become obvious from earlier answers — often it already has.

## What to do with a "no"

Leads that don't qualify shouldn't just get silence. A good qualifying flow closes the loop honestly — pointing a browsing lead toward a self-serve resource, or a too-early lead toward a follow-up in a few months — rather than ghosting them or forcing them onto a sales call that wastes everyone's time.

## Where automation changes the economics

Asking five good questions on every single inbound lead, consistently, at any hour, is a lot to expect from a human team stretched across other work. That consistency — not any single clever question — is where an AI SDR's qualifying conversation earns its keep.
